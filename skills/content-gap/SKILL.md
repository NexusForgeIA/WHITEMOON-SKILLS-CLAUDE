# content-gap

*Cluster: On-page & Content . Part of the 17 SEO Skills set by Vicky Lalwani, Digital Marketing Director.*

## Purpose

Find the specific content the top-ranking pages have that yours lacks, so a page stuck on page two has a concrete, prioritized list of what to add to compete.

## When to use

- A page ranks on page 2-3 and the user wants to know why
- Before writing or updating a page, to see what the SERP expects
- The user asks what topics or questions their page is missing
- Planning a content refresh for an underperforming page

## Inputs required

- Page URL (required)
- Target keyword (required)
- Optional: the specific competitor URLs to compare against

## Step-by-step instructions

1. Identify the target keyword and search intent (informational, commercial, transactional, navigational).
2. Pull the top 10 organic results for that keyword. If competitor URLs are provided, use those; otherwise identify them via search.
3. For each ranking page, extract: main subtopics (H2/H3), entities and terms covered, questions answered, content format (guide, comparison, listicle, tool), and approximate depth/word count.
4. Read the user's page and extract the same dimensions.
5. Build the gap: list every subtopic, question, entity, and format element that appears in the top results but is absent or thin on the user's page. Count how many of the top 10 include each one.
6. Rank the gaps by frequency (covered by 8/10 results = high priority) and tie each to a concrete action: add section, expand section, add table, answer question, add schema.

## Output format

A prioritized gap table: Missing Element | Found in X of top 10 | Type (subtopic/question/entity/format) | Recommended action. Followed by a short brief of the 5 highest-priority additions.

## Quality checklist

- [ ] Search intent is correctly identified before comparing
- [ ] At least 8 of the top 10 results were analyzed
- [ ] Gaps are specific (a named subtopic), not vague ('add more content')
- [ ] Each gap is tied to a concrete action
- [ ] Gaps are ranked by how many top results include them

## Optimization notes

Frequency is the signal. If 9 of 10 ranking pages answer a specific question and yours doesn't, that gap matters far more than something only 2 pages cover. Don't recommend adding everything; recommend matching the consensus of what ranks.
